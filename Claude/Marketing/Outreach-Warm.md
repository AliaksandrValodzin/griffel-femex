# A3 — Warm-intro email

Asset A3 of `FEMEX_GoToMarket.md` §5, for step 0.13: send it to the 20 people from step 0.10
and book 6. **The job of this email is only to get 30 minutes.** It does not pitch, link or
attach anything. The conversation that follows it is run from A4 (`Interview-Guide.md`), not
from a demo.

---

## The email

**Subject:** 30 minutes on how you check a model you didn't build

> Hi [first name],
>
> [One line only you could write — see below.]
>
> I've been building a tool that checks structural models, and before I go further I want
> to understand how engineers actually do it today: when you inherit a model, when one
> changes between issues, or when you check someone else's.
>
> Could I have 30 minutes of your experience? What you check, how long it takes, what's
> gone wrong. There's nothing to prepare and I won't pitch you anything.
>
> A call or a coffee, any time in the next two weeks?
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

**80 words** between greeting and sign-off. With a typical personal line (10–15 words) it
lands at about the 90-word target, and that line is the one people read.

### The personal line

It has to be something only you could write, and it goes first so the email reads as from you
and not from a list. Some examples:

- *"It's been a while since the [project] review. I hope [firm] is treating you well."*
- *"[Mutual contact] said you've been doing a lot of the peer reviews at [firm] lately."*
- *"I still think about the model you inherited on [project] and how long it took to trust it."*

If you can't write one for someone, they probably belong lower down the list of 20, not
higher.

## Follow-up — once, after 5 working days of silence

**Subject:** Re: 30 minutes on how you check a model you didn't build

> Hi [first name] — just bumping this in case it got buried. Happy to fit around you, and
> 20 minutes works too if 30 is a stretch. No worries at all if now's not the time.
>
> [Your name]

Send it once. After that, stop: two unanswered emails is an answer, and a third costs the
relationship more than the conversation is worth.

## When they say yes

> Great, thank you. [Day, time] works for me — [call link / place]. It'll be 30 minutes
> and I'll keep to it.

Then do what the email promised: finish at 30 minutes, and don't demo unless they ask to see
it. If they do ask, that's worth recording on the A4 sheet: "asked to run it on my model,
unprompted" is a row in §4.6.

## Before sending — check each one

- [ ] The personal line is filled in, and specific to this person.
- [ ] Nothing is attached, and there are no links: no repo, no site, no sample report.
- [ ] None of *certify*, *guarantee*, *safe*, *fit for purpose*, *we confirm* (§4.1 rule 1).
      The draft contains none; keep it that way if you edit it.
- [ ] No test counts, rule counts or feature lists (§4.1 rule 2).
- [ ] Sent from your personal address, one at a time, not BCC'd.
- [ ] Recorded on the list of 20: date sent, follow-up due date, outcome.

## Why it's written this way

- **"A tool that checks structural models", said up front.** Hiding that you're building
  something would make the later conversation feel like a bait-and-switch. Naming it once,
  plainly, and then asking about *them* keeps it honest without pitching.
- **The three situations are §8's questions 1–3** (inherit, change between issues, check
  someone else's). The email previews the conversation, so the yes is a yes to the right
  30 minutes.
- **"What's gone wrong"** invites the story. Stories are what the capture sheet needs, and
  they are easier to give than opinions.
- **"I won't pitch you anything"** is a promise about the call, and it is what makes a busy
  senior engineer say yes. Keep it.
- **"The next two weeks"** puts a time frame on it without a deadline, and matches the plan's
  weeks 1–2.
